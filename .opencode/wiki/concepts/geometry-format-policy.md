# Voxels or meshes — the geometry format policy

The repository ships **two** geometry formats and they are not
interchangeable: the world is `.vxw` voxel records, and an *actor* is a
triangle mesh. This page is the rule that decides which one a new object gets,
and the decisions that follow from it.

## The rule: rigid motion vs relative motion

> **A micro-voxel object may be moved and turned as a whole. The moment two
> parts of it must move RELATIVE to each other, it is a mesh actor.**

The tempting wording — "voxels for static, meshes for animated" — is wrong in
both directions. A traced micro object is not frozen: a layer's `behaviour`,
`pos`, `rot` and the live Move/Rotate previews all translate or rotate it, and
that works because the transform is a **single continuous map** applied at
field-sampling time. Nothing about the shape changes, so nothing can tear.

What a traced field cannot do is *deform*: the overlay poses a rest SDF by
mapping each sample back through a bone's inverse transform, and that map is
**discontinuous at every bone seam**. The shipped machinery (claim rule,
no-claimer shell rejection, deep-hit retry) exists to make that
discontinuity survivable, not invisible — see
[[concepts/micro-bone-claim-rule]] and
[[concepts/micro-deformation-surface]]. Legs that swing, a neck that bends and
a tail that sways are exactly the case where the artwork is now a mesh.

So:

| object | format | why |
|---|---|---|
| vase, urn, prop, statue | `.vxw` (micro lattice) | one rigid piece; the tracer gives exact silhouettes and free AO |
| alpaca, any animal or machine with joints | `.stl`/`.obj` mesh | parts move relative to each other; a triangle soup has no world→rest map to tear |

The world's own objects (hamlet buildings, trees, terrain) stay `.vxw` for a
different reason — they are the merged lattice the splat backend renders — and
this policy does not change that.

## What the actor pipeline is

```
model file (Z-up STL)
  → loadMicroMesh(..., UpAxis::Z)      rotation into the engine's Y-up object frame
  → .skin side-car                      DERIVED: per-vertex base bone
  → MicroRig                            DERIVED from the object's own voxels (cached)
  → micro_mesh.vert                     smooth LBS on the GPU, weights recomputed in-shader
  → micro_mesh.frag                     shading sampled in the REST frame
  → fur cards                           grown from the surface, composited over the body
```

Three separate transforms are applied, and their order matters (see
[[concepts/micro-voxel-svo-overlay]]): behaviour (the walk), the user's live
rotation preview, then the staged Move. The shader undoes them in the same
order to reach the baked frame.

## Decisions that are easy to get wrong

### The up axis is declared, not baked

`assets/models/alpaca_mesh.stl` is **Z-up** (a DCC export); the engine's
object frame is **Y-up**. The manifest carries `"meshUp": "z"` (default
`"y"`) and `loadMicroMesh` converts with `(x, y, z) → (x, z, −y)` — a *proper*
rotation (−90° about X, winding preserved).

- Do **not** use `mesh_import`'s `swapYz`: that is `(x, z, y)`, a mirror. A
  mirrored mesh has inverted winding and needs `flip`; the skin and the rig's
  Y-up segments then disagree.
- Convert **before** the min-corner normalisation. `loadMicroMesh` shifts the
  mesh so its AABB min is the object origin; rotating afterwards would compare
  Z-up vertices against Y-up bone capsules and produce a binding that looks
  plausible on screen.
- Verify an asset swap **numerically**, not by eye: rasterise both meshes'
  vertices into a 22³ occupancy grid and take the Jaccard overlap with the
  previous mesh. Measured 2026-10-28: the correct rotation scores 0.828, a
  180° yaw variant 0.156, a y/z mirror 0.158 — the wrong answers are a 5×
  signal, not a coin flip.

### A parsed manifest key must also be written

`writeManifest` rewrites `world.json` from the app's own layer list on every
run. A key that `parseWorldLayer` reads but the writer does not emit survives
exactly one app run. `meshUp` was added to both in the same change;
`behaviour` is the historical example of what this costs (the alpaca stopped
walking). See [[concepts/overlay-silent-write-trap]].

### `.skin` is derived, not authored

The `.skin` side-car is 100% determined by (mesh, rig): it records, per vertex,
the bone the capsule rule assigns. It is regenerated by `vf_micro_skin` and is
**not** a source of truth; treat it like `alpaca.rig` (a cache). A tracked
binary that must match another binary is a drift trap with an unreadable diff
(the swap to the new mesh changed 275,912 lines).

The side-car's per-vertex bone is now only a **fallback** for a mesh with no
rig — the vertex shader computes smooth influences from the kernel, so the
11 MB per-vertex weight buffer is unnecessary.

### The mesh carries no materials — the field's material map is the bridge

An STL has no material assignments (`readMeshFile` defaults every triangle to
rock). The material map comes from the layer's `.vxw` records, but it is
sampled **once per vertex at load** (`assignMicroMeshAppearance`, a 2-cell
`nearestRestSolid` search) — not per fragment. That inversion is the point: the
field's surface and the mesh's surface disagree by up to a cell, so a
per-fragment lookup MISSES near every edge, and a miss has no correct fallback
(`microSurface()` refuses a mesh world) — it renders flat grey.

Measured 2026-10-28 (`vf_micro_mesh_check`, "per-vertex appearance"):

- **54,138 of 54,138 vertices resolved, 0 missed** — the per-vertex material is
  complete for this asset, not a heuristic.
- Cell colour / palette luminance is **1.009 at p1, p50 and p99** (98.2% within
  2%). The records carry **no per-cell tint**, so a material **id** reproduces
  the look and Phase E needs no colour lane.

The same tool reports the mesh↔field agreement, and its bound depends on the
mesh's **provenance**:

| mesh | expected | why |
|---|---|---|
| derived (`*from_voxels*`) | ≥99% | extracted from these cells, so a disagreement is a voxelizer bug |
| authored (the canonical actor) | ≥95% | a triangle surface against a cell set differs by up to a cell along the skin |

Measured 2026-10-28 on `alpaca_mesh.stl`: **96.12%** (151,579 agree, 2,064
mesh-only, 6,121 field-only of 157,700). Still a sharp gate — a wrong axis or
scale collapses it to ~15% (a y/z mirror 15.6%, a 180° yaw 15.8%). The residual
is not noise: **the field's surface sits up to a cell OUTSIDE the mesh on ~4% of
cells**, which is exactly why the coat's roots must come from the MESH (Phase
C) — field-grown roots would be offset by a cell and 1.3% of the surface would
be bald.

### The actor mesh IS closed — and the interesting defect is that it is wound inconsistently

A peer audit on 2026-11-12 reported "7,165 unpaired half-edges, multiplicity
`{1: 39849, 2: 7008, 3: 71, 4: 15}`" and concluded the surface is open. **That
conclusion does not reproduce, and the instrument explains why:** `vf_micro_mesh_check`
now prints the edge census under BOTH keying conventions, and the DIRECTED table
reproduces the audit exactly — `{1: 39849 2: 7008 3: 71 4: 15}`. A directed key
treats the two opposite traversals of one shared edge as two different edges, so
on a closed welded mesh it reports almost every edge unpaired. It is the classic
false positive for "not manifold".

Undirected (the convention culling and root seeding actually care about):

```
edge multiplicity undirected {2: 26897  4: 86}
orientation: 26983 shared edges, 7038 same-direction pairs (26.1 %)
boundary:    0 open edges in 0 loops, total 0.0000 m
```

26,897×2 + 86×4 = **54,138 = every half-edge**, so the count is complete:
**the mesh is closed** — no boundary loops, no holes, nothing for the even-odd
fill to seal that the mesh does not already enclose. Three consequences:

- **Phase C has no fringe hazard from boundaries.** The mesh-seeded coat cannot
  grow cards off an open edge, because there are no open edges. (The audit that
  suggested otherwise is why this section exists — check the keying convention
  before believing any "unpaired edges" number.)
- **The 86 multiplicity-4 edges are NON-MANIFOLD edges (four distinct incident
  faces), not duplicated triangles — and depth ties are therefore IMPOSSIBLE.**
  I first read them as "~28 coincident triangles" by dividing 86 by 3, which is
  exactly the arithmetic-shortcut class that produced the boundary-loop error
  (86 is not even divisible by 3). Counted directly, by comparing each triangle's
  three quantised corners as a set whatever the winding, the tool reports
  **0 repeated triangles, 0 redundant triangles**. So the tie explanation stays
  dead, and the residual after culling is not coincident geometry.
- **The blocking defect for culling is orientation, not openness.** 7,038 shared
  edges (**26.1 %**) are traversed the SAME way by both neighbours, so a quarter
  of the surface disagrees with itself about which side is outside and
  back-face culling deletes an outer wall there. This is the same pathology
  already documented for the old side-car mesh (26,055 of 117,392 shared edges
  same-direction), so it is inherited from the export/voxelization lineage, not
  new. It is the most likely reason culling removed only ~47 % of the
  nondeterminism rather than all of it: the surviving double-cover is
  front-face-on-front-face, and an inverted patch presents a front face where the
  outside should be a back one.

So the ladder is unchanged in conclusion and sharper in reason: the structural
fix (G-buffer as colour attachments, where a failing fragment's framebuffer
writes are suppressed regardless of when the test runs) is the move, culling is a
diagnostic and not a candidate until the winding is repaired, and the winding
itself is a real authoring defect worth a line in a future asset gate.

### Fur influences: two, until a measurement says fourA fur card carries 2 bone indices and 2 weights (record 96 → 112 B). Four would
double a ~120k-card buffer for a seam expected to be sub-millimetre at two.
`VF_TEST_FUR_STAT` is the instrument that decides; it already reports the
shader-vs-rest misclassification and the walk A→B drift.

## Target architecture (and what is done)

The end state is a **self-contained mesh actor**: no field at runtime, so no
field→surface race and no stale ghost of the rest pose.

| phase | change | state |
|---|---|---|
| A | new base mesh, `meshUp` axis, skin rebind | **done** (2026-10-28) |
| B | per-corner material/AO packed in the vertex record (`w` lane: `bone \| mat \| ao \| shadow` as `uintBitsToFloat`); the fragment takes the packed material as PRIMARY and only falls back to the field | **implemented** (2026-11-12): CPU pass + vertex shader + fragment hook, unit tests green; `test-micro` still needs one clean run (two runs voided, see below) |
| C | fur cards grown from the MESH surface (area-weighted roots), same bone blend | planned (blocked on the fur shader work) |
| D | pick a mover on its posed mesh (`deformedVerts`) + a shadow decal for a rotating actor | planned |
| E | retire the actor's `.vxw` field; retain it only for static (traced) objects — **no colour lane needed** (see the tint measurement above) | planned |
| F | delete the tracer's bone/claim machinery; keep the rigid transform chain (`microMapWorld`, `microShadeToBaked`, `microDirToBaked`) | planned |

Phase B's encoding lives in ONE place, `meshVertexWord` in
`src/voxel/micro_mesh.hpp` (byte 0 bone+1, 1 material with 255 = "look it up",
2 AO, 3 reserved); `shaders/micro_mesh.vert` decodes it with
`floatBitsToUint` and passes `flat out uint vWord` + an interpolated `vAo`. The
material byte must be **flat** (a triangle spanning a seam would blend two
palettes) while AO is continuous. A zero vertex record decodes as bone 0 /
material 0, which is why the loader writes 255 for "unresolved" rather than
leaving the lane zero.

`micro_mesh.frag` consumes **byte 1 only**, as the primary appearance:
`packedMat = (vWord >> 8) & 0xFF`, and when it is a legal palette index the
albedo/reflection come straight from `kPalette`/`kMatRefl` — no octree descent,
no per-fragment miss. `kPaletteN` is now named in `common_base.glsl` as well as
`common.hpp`, and the byte is range-checked against it, so an out-of-range
material can only fall back, never index past the array. The AO byte stays
unconsumed on purpose: the lighting's AO is still the shader's `microAO` march,
and replacing it with the per-vertex value is a look change that needs its own
measurement, not a free win.

Two facts make the phases safe to stage:

- **The field is still the shading source**, so B and C must land before E.
- **The old per-fragment fallback could not succeed**: `microSurface()` skips
  `microWorldIsMesh` worlds, so a material lookup that missed rendered flat grey
  (albedo 0.5 / refl 0.15 / rough 0.9). That is why the material had to become
  a load-time, per-vertex fact; the field lookup is now only a safety net for a
  vertex that found no solid rest cell, and the shipped asset has none.

### The raster pass must early-test, or its own far wall decides the pixel

The mesh pipeline runs with **culling off** (an exporter's winding can disagree,
and a wrongly-culled animal loses a limb), and the fragment shader writes the
G-buffer with `imageStore` **side effects**. Those two facts combine badly: a
fragment shader with storage writes forces the depth test **late**, so on a
closed surface both the near-wall and the far-wall fragment of a pixel execute,
**both store**, and the depth test then decides only which *framebuffer* output
survives — it cannot unstore the loser. The surviving pixel is whichever
fragment stored last, and that order is undefined.

Measured 2026-11-12 in one session, one binary, one camera, fur off, behaviour
frozen: **traced (`VF_MESH=0`) pair = 0 px; raster pair = 4,278 px, max delta
141** (the near wall is lit, the far wall is in shadow). That single fork
exonerated the world backend (`--mode svo` flickered too), the GPU cull pre-pass,
the Hi-Z pyramid, and the frames-in-flight class (`VF_TEST_SERIAL_SUBMIT` did
nothing) — and it explains why the barrier fix and the per-slot depth fix both
failed to help: they ordered the wrong thing.

**CONFIRMATION FAILED (2026-11-12): the mode is INERT on this driver.** With the
declaration compiled in (`spirv-dis` shows `OpExecutionMode %main
EarlyFragmentTests`, `--selftest` passes, same binary, same camera, arms in one
session, animal verified present), the raster `VF_FUR=0` pair measured **4,653 px
against its 4,278 px baseline** and the fur-ON shipping arm **3,980 px against
4,533** — the flicker did not move. That is the price of a mode with no feature
bit: nothing at runtime says whether the driver honoured it, and only the
duplicate pair can tell you. It honoured the declaration and ignored the
semantics, which is the response a storage-writing fragment shader gets here.

**But the magnitude is not yet a pinned quantity, and that undermines every
~4.5 k px figure above.** A controlled A/B of the depth-read (`snapshot` =
binding 41 vs `live` = binding 13) at `fur_check`'s alpaca camera, 900x700,
behaviour frozen, `--animtime 0`, presence grepped 3/3 in every render and the
raster path confirmed in the log (`raster-owned from alpaca_mesh.stl (54138
verts, 18046 tris)`, `world 2 raster-owned`), measured:

```
snapshot  pair: 17 px > 6/255, max 25
live      pair: 18 px > 6/255, max 29
```

Two findings. (1) **The snapshot buys nothing measurable** — 17 vs 18 px is the
same number, so the read path owns none of the residual and the retracted
"~89 % removed" ratio is now retracted with data behind it. (2) **17-18 px is not
~4.3 k px, and it is not the 24,427 px `fur_check` reports at the same camera
with the same settings.** The cross-arm comparison in that run is void because a
peer build relinked `build/voxelforge` between the two arms, so the arms were not
measured on one binary — the within-arm pairs are still valid, and they say the
mesh is nearly deterministic AS INVOKED HERE.

**THE CAMERA IS THE VARIABLE — AND THE MECHANISM I CALLED "STRONGLY SUPPORTED"
IS FALSIFIED.** A follow-up sweep (one binary `09a70035`, one session, same env,
byte-identical freeze spec, only the viewpoint changed) measured **5,147 px** at
the hand-pinned flicker camera `-4.9 1.25 20.6 → -6.32 0.75 19.2` — reproducing
the 4,278 / 4,653 / 4,955 family — against **15 px** (max 62) at
`fur_check`'s `alpaca_cam()`. Seven poses at the flicker camera span 3,891-5,147,
a ±15 % band whose extremes come from the freeze string's `clipTime` slot, not
from gait: **pose is nearly irrelevant, the view is essentially everything.**
Three numbers that looked like contradictions are now consistent: my
17-18 px was real at a viewpoint where the bug barely bites; `fur_check`'s
24,427 px is the walking-animal artifact (`fur_check.render()` passes no
`VF_TEST_BEHAVIOUR_FREEZE`, so its noise floor straddles wall-clock motion); and
the ~4.3-5 k family reproduces at the flicker camera. **But "divergence is
proportional to double-covered area" is dead as written:** the CPU rasteriser
counts 31,449 doubled px at the flk camera against 23,935 at the fur camera — a
ratio of **1.31** — where the divergence ratio is **343**. So the 5,147 : 15 split
is recorded as **the observation any mechanism must explain**, not as a
prediction that was borne out. The leading refinement was NEAR-TIES: an unordered
store only decides a pixel when its two candidate surfaces are close enough that
`LESS` is settled by interpolation noise rather than geometry, and two walls
centimetres apart head-on converge edge-on — which is precisely a two-camera
difference. The census was pre-declared: land near 343 and the mechanism closes
quantitatively; land near 1.3 and the double-cover family is wrong.

**THE CENSUS RAN, AND THREE PREDICTIONS ARE DEAD (2026-11-12).** Near-tie
counts at the flk camera against the fur camera are **10 / 24 / 1,423 px** at
1 / 5 / 50 mm gaps versus **1 / 10 / 303** — ratios 10.0 / 2.4 / 4.7 against the
343 split. The shape is right (the ratio grows as the gap tightens) and the size
is two orders of magnitude short. Two further negatives from the same
investigation: the *disagreement* magnitude does not select the pixels — changed
in both of two independent pairs has a median luma delta of **27.0** against
**24.0** for changed in only one arm, a 12 % gap on a scale where the near and
far walls differ by ~140 — and the count repeats while the *set* moves (Jaccard
**0.237 / 0.231 / 0.229**, where a deterministic cause scores ≈1.0 and chance
over the ~31 k px body scores ≈0.084). That set test is what keeps the
attachment fix worth doing: it establishes the failure is genuinely unordered,
which is the fix's premise, while refusing every size model built on it. What
survives is **eligibility** — which pixels can be written twice at all: ~5 k at
one viewpoint, ~15 at another, no geometric count tracking the difference. The
cull fork (4,955 → 2,345 px) still proves double cover contributes; it just does
not size the contribution. Recorded as the observation any mechanism must
explain, and explicitly not as a law to fit. Details:
[[concepts/render-nondeterminism]].

Two rules follow. A determinism gate must measure `0 px` **where the bug is
strong** — at `alpaca_cam()` it would pass at 15 px with the bug fully present,
which is `fur_check`'s failure mode repeated; use `-4.9 1.25 20.6 -6.32 0.75
19.2` as the flicker camera. And the freeze string's slot order is
`name:dx,dy,dz:heading:gait:graze:clipTime` — a value in the wrong slot parses
without complaint and means something else, which is how a day of "frozen"
figures here were actually a symmetric standing stance.

**AND THE SNAPSHOT CONTRIBUTES NOTHING MEASURABLE (2026-11-12, controlled A/B).**
One binary per arm, one camera (`fur_check`'s alpaca view at 900x700), behaviour
frozen, `--animtime 0`, presence grepped every render (3/3), raster path
confirmed in the log (`raster-owned from alpaca_mesh.stl (54138 verts, 18046
tris)`), and only the depth-read image swapped:

```
snapshot  pair: 17 px > 6/255, max 25, meanabs 0.0004
live      pair: 18 px > 6/255, max 29, meanabs 0.0004
verdict: no measurable difference
```

Two conclusions. First, the binding-41 snapshot buys nothing at this operating
point — the read hazard was real, but its residual contribution is zero, which
retires the "~89 % removed" ratio for good. Second, and more uncomfortable:
**17-18 px on a self-overlapping raster mesh is not ~4.3 k px**, and it is not
the 24,427 px `fur_check` reports at the same camera with the same settings. So
the magnitude is set by the INVOCATION, not by the code, and no ~4.5 k px figure
in this page is a property of the mesh until the invocation is pinned. Suspect
#1: `fur_check.render()` sets only `VF_OVERLAY_PATH` plus its own extras — no
`VF_TEST_BEHAVIOUR_FREEZE` — so its noise-floor pair straddles wall-clock animal
motion, which AGENTS already documents as non-reproducible for a headless shot of
a walking animal. If that is the cause, the gate is measuring the alpaca taking a
step, not a GPU race, and the fix belongs in the harness. The next measurement is
a ladder over freeze / `VF_NO_OVERLAY` / `VF_FUR` / camera.

So the line stays as documentation of intent, NOT as a cure, and the bug is
OPEN. Two further CPU audits on the same asset reshape the ladder: the winding
argument against culling is FALSE for this mesh (**18,046/18,046** triangles
agree with the stored normal, and the loader's `(x,y,z) → (x,z,−y)` is a proper
rotation so it survives), but the surface is **not closed** — **7,165 unpaired
half-edges**, multiplicity `{1: 39849, 2: 7008, 3: 71, 4: 15}` — so culling the
back walls would show the interior through the openings; and there are **0
duplicate triangles and 0 coplanar pairs**, so a depth-tie explanation is dead.
The remaining fork is an env-gated `VK_CULL_MODE_BACK_BIT` diagnostic
(`VF_MESH_CULL=1`): 0 px there convicts the double-covered store and makes the
structural fix necessary — writing the G-buffer as colour attachments instead of
`imageStore`, which makes the depth test authoritative by construction rather
than by a mode the driver may ignore. Still ~4 k px and the double-wall theory
dies with it.

Two conclusions that are safe to hold regardless: the FORK is real (traced 0 px
vs raster ~4 k px, so the raster pass owns the divergence, and the Phase B
material lane is exonerated because it is hdr-only and the traced path shares
every shading term), and a rule of general reach: **any pass that writes the
G-buffer as a side effect inherits this hazard, and declaring early tests is not
a fix you can trust without a duplicate-render pair.**



### The material-seam census: 1.98 % of triangles, all of them at the coat edge

Making the material per VERTEX and `flat` buys "never misses" at a price: a
triangle whose three vertices resolve to different materials renders as ONE
material (the provoking vertex's), so a material boundary becomes triangle-shaped
instead of cell-shaped. On the alpaca the eyes, muzzle and hooves ride other
materials (4 rock, 19 thatch) inside the fleece, so the number that decides
whether the lane is free is how many of the 18,046 triangles span a boundary.

Measured 2026-11-12 (`vf_micro_mesh_check`, "per-vertex appearance"):
**17,688 uniform, 358 material-seam (1.98 %), 358 coat-against-bare, 0
unresolved.** Every seam triangle is a fleece-against-skin one, and there is not
one seam between two bare materials — the cost is entirely the coat's outline
against eyes, muzzle and hooves.

So the price of the `flat` lane is exactly that: on 1.98 % of the surface the
boundary takes the provoking vertex's material instead of the per-pixel cell,
shifting an eye or hoof outline by at most ONE triangle edge (~2–4 mm on a 30 cm
animal at this tessellation). What it buys is the removal of the failure class —
a per-fragment miss has no correct fallback and renders flat grey. Accepted
cost, not a defect, and it is the number confirming Phase E still needs no colour
lane.

If a human ever sees the shift, the escape hatch is already provisioned and costs
no extra lane: set **bit 0 of byte 3** (currently reserved) as a per-vertex
"this triangle spans a seam" flag and let ONLY those ~2 % of fragments take the
field lookup, keeping the exact boundary where it matters and the free palette
read everywhere else.

## Gates

- **A determinism bug cannot be caught by a correctness gate.** `micro_check`
  PASSES on a binary that still flickers by ~4.8 k px, because every check it
  makes (mean colour, silhouette fill, shadow verdicts) is satisfied by either
  member of the pair. The arbiter for this class is a duplicate-render pair —
  `fur_check`'s noise-floor check — so a red noise floor with everything else
  green is the expected shape of this bug, not a contradiction.
- `tests/test_micro.cpp` — the SDF sign/quantum/margin contract, the CPU trace,
  the pivot invariance of a micro rotation, and the fur ownership rule.
- `tests/micro_check.py` — object visible, material bright/neutral, silhouette
  not a filled rectangle, micro shadows micro, world view unchanged, SVO
  parity, and (since 2026-10-28) that a Z-up asset stands upright at headings
  0/90/180/270.
- `tests/fur_check.py` — noise floor, signal, confinement, shape,
  backend-independence, and the `VF_FUR=0` escape hatch.
- `python3 tests/visual_check.py` — the world is untouched by an actor change.
